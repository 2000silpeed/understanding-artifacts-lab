# Correlation is not causation

## hook
If ice cream sales rise when pool visits rise, [#question] did one cause the other? This is synthetic data.

## pattern
[#mix] Mixing warm and cool days makes both values rise. [#pattern] The chart can show correlation, even without a direct cause.

## dag
[#common] Temperature is the common cause. [#arrows] It points to ice cream sales, and to pool visits and accident risk.

## missing
[#graph] The graph has two arrows from temperature. [#missing] It has no arrow from sales to the outcome. Observation cannot add that arrow.

## group
[#hold] Hold temperature at twenty two degrees. [#group] The right chart keeps one group, and the rising cloud fades.

## intervention
[#change] Now change ice cream sales while temperature stays fixed. [#outcome] The outcome rule stays separate. This is a simulation, not real people.

## questions
[#observe] Observation asks whether large sales appear with large outcomes. [#intervene] Intervention asks whether changing sales changes outcomes under stated conditions. They need different evidence.

## outro {hold=0.7}
[#controls] Switch views and reset the example. Temperature here is simulated. [#lesson] Correlation can start a question, but causation needs design and assumptions.
